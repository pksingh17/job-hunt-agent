# Company and employee-review research

The goal is a fair picture of what working there is like, with an honest label on how much you actually know.

## Sources, in rough order of value
1. The region pack's review sites (for example AmbitionBox in India, Kununu in Germany, Glassdoor almost everywhere).
2. Reddit and regional forums, searched as `"<company>" reviews`, `"<company>" salary delay`, `"<company>" layoffs`.
3. News from the last 18 months: layoffs, funding, lawsuits, acquisitions, leadership churn.
4. The company's own careers page, LinkedIn page and headcount trend.
5. The job board's own reviews section if it has one.

## Method
- Read at least eight to ten reviews where they exist, newest first.
- A pattern is the same complaint from several people across more than one source or more than one year. One furious review is noise.
- Weight the last 24 months most. Old complaints about a past management team matter less.
- Note the reviewers' function when visible. Engineering may love a place that treats marketing badly.
- Ratings alone tell you little. Read the text.

## Strong red flags
Repeated reports of: salary delays or unpaid salary, misleading JDs (job turns out to be sales or cold calling), employment bonds or exit penalties, unpaid overtime or surprise weekend work, retained documents or certificates, extreme turnover, systematic lack of growth, harassment that management ignored, "pay a fee to apply" or "pay for training".

Any fee demanded from the candidate is close to a certain scam. Mark it, score Company 0, and SKIP.

## Evidence levels
Record one in the tracker's `Review Evidence` column:
- **Strong**: many recent reviews across two or more sources, plus news or public info
- **Moderate**: a reasonable number of reviews on one source, or a mix of thin sources that agree
- **Thin**: a handful of reviews, or the main review site blocked automated access
- **None**: nothing readable found

When a site blocks you, say "review site blocked automated access" in the summary. Never fill the gap with guesses.

## Writing the review summary
Two or three sentences in the tracker: the dominant pattern, the strongest positive, and any recurring red flag, with the evidence level implied by the wording. Put red flags in their own column so they can be filtered.
